## 2. Verified External Sources

### Source 1: OpenStax College Physics (2e)
- **Title:** *College Physics 2e* — Chapter 4: Dynamics: Force and Newton's Laws of Motion, Section 4.4: "Newton’s Third Law of Motion: Symmetry in Forces"
- **Authors / Organization:** OpenStax, Rice University (Paul Peter Urone, Roger Hinrichs, et al.)
- **URL:** [https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion-symmetry-in-forces](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/4-4-newtons-third-law-of-motion-symmetry-in-forces)
- **Claims Supported:**
  - Newton's Third Law mathematical definition: $\vec{F}_{\text{A on B}} = -\vec{F}_{\text{B on A}}$ (equal magnitude, opposite direction).
  - Forces as mutual interactions between two bodies.
  - Action-reaction pairs act on *different objects*, explaining why they do not cancel out on a single object.
  - Integration with Newton's Second Law ($F = ma$) to explain differing accelerations in collisions (e.g., unequal masses).

### Source 2: NASA Glenn Research Center — Beginner's Guide to Rockets
- **Title:** "Newton's Third Law of Motion" & "Rocket Thrust"
- **Organization:** National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA Glenn Research Center)
- **URLs:**
  - [https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/newton3.html](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/newton3.html)
  - [https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/rocket/rockth.html](https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/rocket/rockth.html)
- **Claims Supported:**
  - Rocket propulsion in a vacuum: Rockets do not need air or an atmosphere to push against. Thrust is generated because the rocket expels exhaust gases backward (action force), and the expanding gases exert an equal and opposite reaction force on the rocket, accelerating it forward in space.
