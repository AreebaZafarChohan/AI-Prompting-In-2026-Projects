# Chapter Evaluation Rubric

This evaluation assesses `project-4/chapter/draft.md` against the project requirements, target audience (high school students), pedagogical approach (Option C: Predict, Test, Explain), and chosen outline (Outline 2: Guided Inquiry — Prediction + Light Visual Support).

---

## 1. Core Evaluation Criteria

### 1. Clarity
- **Score:** 8 / 10
- **Evidence:** The text uses direct, conversational explanations and step-by-step logic (e.g., Section 2: "You cannot push on something without it pushing back on you"; Section 4 clearly contrasts forces on the same object vs. forces on different objects).
- **Weakness:** Section 3 introduces vector notation ($\vec{F}_{\text{A on B}} = -\vec{F}_{\text{B on A}}$) abruptly without explicitly defining what the subscript notation represents to a beginner.
- **Smallest Useful Improvement:** Add a brief one-line explanation breaking down the notation ($A \text{ on } B$ means the force exerted by object A onto object B, and the negative sign indicates opposite direction).

### 2. Accuracy
- **Score:** 9 / 10
- **Evidence:** Correctly defines forces as simultaneous mutual interactions between two bodies, accurately resolves the non-cancellation paradox via separate system boundaries, and correctly uses Newton's Second Law ($a = F/m$) to explain unequal damage/acceleration in collisions.
- **Weakness:** In Section 3, the phrase "For every action force, there is an equal and opposite reaction force" is listed without immediately clarifying that "action" and "reaction" are simultaneous rather than sequential causes and effects (though "Simultaneous" is listed in the bullet points below, the colloquial phrasing can still mislead).
- **Smallest Useful Improvement:** Explicitly note next to the classic phrasing that "action" and "reaction" happen at the exact same moment.

### 3. Age-fit
- **Score:** 8 / 10
- **Evidence:** Tone and relatable scenarios (skateboards, highway truck vs. mosquito, walking) fit high school students without sounding overly childish or mathematically intimidating.
- **Weakness:** Checkpoint question 3 asks the student to "Draw an interaction diagram" even though the chapter provided no visual diagrams or examples of what an interaction diagram looks like.
- **Smallest Useful Improvement:** Include a simple text/ASCII or structured visual representation of an interaction pair so students have a model to follow.

### 4. Usefulness for Revision
- **Score:** 4 / 10
- **Evidence:** Section 6 contains 3 conceptual questions for self-testing.
- **Weakness:** The chapter completely lacks structured revision tools (no recap summary, no flashcard definitions, no quiz with answer keys/explanations, and no multi-day study/revision plan).
- **Smallest Useful Improvement:** Add a concise recap box, flashcards for key terms, a short quiz with answers, and a 7-day revision schedule at the end of the chapter.
